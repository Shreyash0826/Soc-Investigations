# 🛡️ Incident Analysis Report: Tusk Infostealer Campaign Analysis

## 1. Executive Summary
This incident report documents a detailed technical analysis of the **Tusk Infostealer** campaign, a specialized cyber-espionage and financial theft operation targeting cryptocurrency users within the MultiversX (formerly Elrond) ecosystem. The threat actors employed typosquatted domain infrastructure mimicking Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO) management platforms and fake AI translation software to deliver second-stage infostealers (**StealC** and **DanaBot**).

The analysis covers initial sample metadata, cloud staging mechanisms, static binary config extraction, network C2 infrastructure mapping, and extracted indicators of compromise (IOCs).

## 3. Attack Vector & Sub-Campaign Infrastructure

### A. Phase 1: DAO Management Typosquatting
The campaign initially relied on spear-phishing and drive-by downloads via social engineering. Operators registered typosquatted domain names mimicking legitimate MultiversX Web3 platforms:
* **Legitimate Platform:** `peerme.io` (MultiversX DAO Creation & Governance)
* **Malicious Phishing Domain:** `tidyme.io` (Attacker-controlled Clone)

### B. Phase 2: AI Translator Spoofing Sub-Campaign
In a subsequent operational phase, the threat group launched a secondary campaign targeting users seeking automated translation tools:
* **Legitimate Service:** `yous.ai`
* **Malicious Counterpart:** `voico.io`

## 6. MITRE ATT&CK Alignment

* **[T1566.002] Phishing: Spearphishing Link:** Exploitation of typosquatted domains (`tidyme.io`, `voico.io`) to trick victims.
* **[T1105] Ingress Tool Transfer:** Fetching malicious payloads staged on `Dropbox` infrastructure.
* **[T1140] Deobfuscate/Decode Files or Information:** Decoding base64 configuration data and extracting encrypted archives via `newfile2024`.
* **[T1041] Exfiltration Over C2 Channel:** Transmitting harvested credentials and wallet data to external StealC IP addresses (`46.8.238.240`, `23.94.225.177`).
